#  Cover letter

I am versed in Javascript ES6, MongoDB databases, locally and in the cloud, Node, Express, React.js and Material-UI. My first language was Python until I decided to change to Web Development. I am in the pursue of a bachelor's degree in linguistics, and consider this to be one of my strengths, first because of the many correlations between the study of linguistics and computer programming, and second becaus of the many products that I know will result from combining linguistics with coding. I can see that both fields are able to profit from the other and I have two practical examples to support my theory - the first is the app Noam, which you'll see described bellow; the second, that is just an idea for now, is a new UI/UX for media streaming apps based on the intrinsic knowledge that humans have about prepositions. 

So, I taught myself how to code using books and video tutorials and lots of practice. I've been studying for hours a day since the pandemic period started, and I've already created some projects by myself, which you can check at my github repository. I'm a lifelong linux user, my first install was RedHat 6 when I was still 15, and since then I mostly used this OS. I spent some years with Debian and Ubuntu until I finally settled with Archlinux five years ago. Operating my system through the terminal is nowadays something I do not only because it's better in many ways but also for fun. I am new to coding, but I got the gist and I'm aware of where my focus should be: Javascript essentials (I'm still reading The Definitive Guide 2020) and Design Patterns, which I've been studying with conferences made by Robert C. Martin, aka "Uncle Bob".

Before I switched to coding, my career was supported on the tripod of linguistics services: teaching, proofreading and translation. Again, I see this as positive value in my new career, because I believe that I'm neat with my code (I love refactoring) due to those many years correcting every tiny detail of spelling or grammar in my own texts or in the text of others. Also, I have taught English to Portuguese speakers for 10 years, and in 2019 and 2020 I got the chance to teach English inside startup companies, where I had the opportunity to learn about Agile, Scrum, Kanban and a little bit about how the daily life of a developer is like. Besides that, I have two books translated from English to Portuguese, one is by the famous british author, Terry Pratchet. I also know a little bit of Spanish, if that matters.

My flagship project is a fullstack web application, a social media clone that offers a timeline for posts with likes and comments and user following made with the MERN stack: a complete Express backend server that has user authentication, using JWT, and offers RESTful API endpoints that connects to a MongoDB cloud-based database and to a React frontend styled with Material-UI. 

I've made some other projects, all JS based: A Drum simulator that can be played with the keyboard; a clone of the classic mobile game Snake; An analog clock; a calculator; a 3D simulation of our solar system, made with the library three.js. All these projects are deployed to Netlify through a connection with my github repositories and reflect the current developing stage of each of them.

I have two projects that are still raw in their development stages: The first is Noam, a syntax tree generator aimed at high school students. Syntax Tree theory, created by the linguist Noam Chomsky, is a modern way of studying how natural languages are structured, using graphics (similar to mindmaps) to explain relations between elements of a sentence. Right now it's made in pure js but I'm refactoring it to use three.js possibilities to offer a more intense and intuitive UI/UX experience for teenagers.

The second project is Papyrus, A Game Master Screen emulator for tabletop RPG offline sessions, which will be another fullstack implementation. I have a skeleton made but I'm still considering features users would like to see in it.

My goal as a developer is to write code in Javascript language mostly, but I like Python too (actually I miss this language a bit). I enjoy the puzzle-solving feeling when I'm coding, it feels like I'm gaming, actually. Also, I enjoy tidying up my code after it's working. Actually, I feel an urge to do so, because leaving the code in its initial form, often a mess, is a too heavy cognitive load to bear. I get anxious leaving that way, so I prefer to refactor it to modules, improve variable names, 'hide' implementations inside higher order functions and so on. I want to project stable APIs and create engaging user interfaces for all kind of applications. However, I realized that my natural creativity tends towards apps with educational purposes. Being a teacher for so long  makes me daydream about useful apps for learning, like Noam. The solar system I'm creating will also have a purpose related to education.

I don't consider any of my projects as finished. I can see improvements, new features and even some better refactoring to do in all of them. I consider myself as a constant ongoing development, but I am on my way, always looking forward, with my experience helping me in crossroad decisions. I already have some ideas for my career development: As I am choosing topics to study according to my needs, unless a paid job requires me to learn something else, I intend to keep improving my skills with three.JS, but mixing it with some Blender and Godot. Not for the creation of actual games, but to create 3D gamified web apps that could also be deployed to AR and VR solutions. I also have a steadfast interest in non-financial blockchain technologies and what they can offer us in terms of more just and peaceful social contracts, but in this path I've just had my first steps.

In parallel to my career in web development, I'll keep teaching, although changing subjects: instead of teaching a natural language, I plan to start teaching programming to a different audience: my people of the linguistics field, and also social scientists, sociologists, psychologists and other professionals of human sciences, because I strongly believe that this will yield good results. Later on, I'll be teaching code for teenagers and children, connecting this subject to the gaming experience and toh the reasoning they have to do to study their mother language.

Finally, on a personal note, I am a student of two eastern disciplines: I practice the Japanese martial arts called Aikido and I do Yoga at home. I am also a science fiction writer (in Portuguese, unfortunately) and an amateur musician, playing the guitar and the transversal flute.
